# Push readiness, run 1: Jev, 2026-10-12

First live run of the two sets in [`policy/push.yaml`](../../policy/push.yaml) against
`jev-1.13.0`, over the fixtures that ship with them. The answers are committed beside this
file and re-score with no key:

```bash
node bin/bouncer.cjs calibrate --policy policy/push.yaml --set push --fixtures fixtures/push.jsonl --from docs/calibration/2026-10-12-push-1.jsonl
node bin/bouncer.cjs calibrate --policy policy/push.yaml --set push_file --fixtures fixtures/push-file.jsonl --from docs/calibration/2026-10-12-push-file-1.jsonl
```

Questions fingerprints: `push` `1148-286e764a`; `push_file` is on every line of its file.

**What this is evidence of, and what it is not.** The 26 fixtures were written by the same
hand, in the same hour, as the questions. A good score says the wording reads the way it was
meant to. It says nothing about real pushes, where the baseline is that almost all of them
are fine and the number that matters is how often this holds one that was. That evidence is
`readiness.jsonl` compared with CI, and none exists yet.

## Result

| set | question | n | accuracy at 0.5 | Brier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `push` | `message_mismatch` | 8 | 100% | 0.004 |
| `push` | `untested_behaviour` | 9 | 89% | 0.054 |
| `push_file` | `debug_leftover` | 10 | 100% | 0.007 |
| `push_file` | `unfinished` | 9 | 100% | 0.014 |

At the policy's thresholds (0.70 everywhere, no uncertainty band) the verdict matched the
label on 25 of 26 fixtures: no fixture labelled fine was held, and one labelled as needing a
hold was let through.

## The one it missed

`default-changed-no-test`: a commit called "chore: tidy the upload settings" that raises an
upload limit and turns anonymous uploads on, with no test. `untested_behaviour` read 0.31.
The question's `false` side lists "configuration or build settings", and two changed
constants read as configuration. That reading is defensible, and it is the case the label
exists to catch, so the wording was left alone rather than tuned until one fixture passed.
It is the first thing to look at when real pushes say where this question is wrong.

## Things the answers show that the verdicts do not

- `unfinished` reads 0.45 to 0.86 on the five debug-statement fixtures it is not labelled
  on (a `console.log`, a breakpoint, `.only`, a commented-out block, a sleep). Those hold on
  `debug_leftover` anyway, so no verdict changes, but the two questions overlap: a leftover
  debug statement is, to this model, also unfinished work. If one of them is ever dropped or
  moved to a different threshold, that overlap is what moves with it.
- Every fixture labelled fine scores 0.24 or lower on both file questions and 0.06 or lower
  on both push questions. There is room under 0.70.
